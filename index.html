Russia is supplying missiles to Iran as Tehran rebuilds its arsenal, report says

A Reuters investigation traces how Iran keeps rebuilding missile and drone stocks through a 5,000-agency procurement network importing at least $2 billion of military-related goods since January 2025, despite the US naval blockade — the latest evidence of Tehran rebuilding its arsenal.

Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/russia-iran-missiles-rebuild/

What happened

Russia is supplying Iran with missiles as Tehran rebuilds its arsenal, according to Bloomberg reporting cited by Haaretz on October 10, 2026. The reporting states Iran retains production capacity and substantial ballistic missile stocks despite eight months of US-Israeli airstrikes.

What is verified

  • Bloomberg reporting, cited by Haaretz (Oct 10, 2026), says Russia is supplying missiles to Iran.
  • The reporting states Iran retains ballistic missile production capacity and substantial stocks.

What remains disputed

The types, quantities, and delivery timeline of the missiles have not been publicly specified. The claim that eight months of strikes failed to degrade Iran's missile capability will be contested by US and Israeli officials; no official US/Israeli assessment is reflected in the cited reporting. Russia's strategic intent — defensive resupply vs. deepening war partnership — is unstated. The K-10 documents (v17) are published primary records corroborated by an FT investigation; the newsroom has not independently authenticated them, and no Russian or Iranian confirmation appears in the accounts checked. Dallas Analytics' suggestion that the Izdeliye 347 ground launcher was adapted for Iran is analysis, not established fact, and no finished LMUR missile delivery is documented in this material.

Why it matters

If accurate, this is the clearest evidence yet that the US-Israeli air campaign has not achieved its stated aim of degrading Iran's strike capability — and that Moscow is willing to backfill it. That combination hardens Iran's negotiating position and lengthens the war's expected runway.

Strategic context

Russia and Iran deepened military cooperation after 2022, most visibly through Iranian drones in Ukraine. The reverse flow — Russian munitions into Iran — marks a new phase. It follows the Putin–Pezeshkian meeting in Ashgabat, where Moscow pledged mediation while visibly tightening its embrace of Tehran.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: this is routine alliance maintenance — Moscow resupplying a partner to keep the war grinding and keep Washington distracted. Reading two: it is a deterrent signal to Washington and Jerusalem ahead of any escalation. Reading three (skeptical): the reporting may overstate deliveries or conflate agreements with delivered hardware; the physical evidence is not public.

Trump and Putin struck a diesel deal to blunt Iran-war fuel prices

Over 300,000 metric tons of Russian fuel would flow under a temporary US sanctions lift, easing the price shock from the war on Iran — but analysts doubt it will be enough.

Updated 05 Nov 2026, 12:02 UTC Verified 115 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/diesel-deal-iran-war/

What happened

The Trump administration and Russia have struck an arrangement to supply more than 300,000 metric tons of Russian diesel to the US and global markets, according to Haaretz reporting published October 10, 2026. The deal involves a temporary US lift of sanctions on Russian fuel products. Diesel prices fell after the announcement.

What is verified

  • Brent $103.35 (-1.36%), WTI $90.42 (-1.60%) on Trump's energy-ceasefire announcement (Economic Times, Oct 12)
  • Zelenskyy awaiting details; Russia had not commented (Economic Times, Oct 12)

What remains disputed

Analysts cited in the reporting doubt the volume will be enough to materially calm fuel markets. The exact duration of the sanctions carve-out, the delivery timeline, and how the fuel will be routed into US supply chains remain unclear. Whether this marks a broader policy shift toward Russia or a one-off wartime measure is not established. Rubio on India not being able to pivot from Russian oil overnight (BusinessToday — attributed) Trump claim he is negotiating a Russia-Ukraine peace deal and that Moscow has not helped Iran during the war (BusinessToday — attributed)

Why it matters

Energy is the second front of the Iran war. With airstrikes disrupting supply routes and insurance markets jittery since February 2026, fuel prices feed directly into inflation — a political problem months before the US midterms. A US–Russia energy deal, even temporary, would have been unthinkable a year ago; its arrival signals how acute the price pressure has become.

Strategic context

Since strikes on Iran began on February 28, 2026, global diesel and distillate markets have tightened. Russia — under Western sanctions since 2022 — remains one of the world's largest diesel exporters, with volumes effectively locked out of Western markets. The Trump administration has publicly pursued a pragmatic, deal-driven approach to Moscow throughout 2026, making energy the latest testing ground.

Competing interpretations

One reading: this is pure price management — a tactical wartime fix with no strategic meaning. Another: it is the thin end of sanctions normalization, giving Moscow a sanctioned-product channel back into Western markets. A third, more skeptical reading: the volume is symbolic, designed to move headlines and futures prices rather than physical supply. The evidence so far supports the first reading, but the structure of the sanctions carve-out will be the tell.

Treasury license would let Russian diesel flow through April 2027

The diesel deal's sanctions carve-out reportedly extends through April 2027 — turning a wartime fix into a year-plus arrangement.

Updated 27 Oct 2026, 03:34 UTC Verified 14 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/treasury-license-russian-diesel/

What happened

This is a developing-story monitor. The thread: the Treasury license underpinning the Trump–Putin diesel arrangement reportedly runs through April 2027 — well past the US midterms and deep into the war's expected runway. Awaiting verification: the license text itself.

What is verified

  • OFAC general license authorizes Russian-diesel sale/delivery/offloading/import through 12:01 am EDT, April 7, 2027 (The Hindu/PTI, Oct 10, 2026).
  • The license excludes debits to accounts of Russia's central bank, national wealth fund, and finance ministry at US institutions (The National, Oct 9, 2026).

What remains disputed

The exact expiry date, the scope of products covered, renewal terms, and whether Congress or allies were consulted. An 18-month carve-out is a different animal than a 90-day emergency waiver — the difference matters. The legal validity of OFAC General License No. 135 is now openly contested: House Judiciary Ranking Member Raskin argues (Oct 26) it cannot override the 2022 statutory ban; no court ruling on the license's validity appears in the material reviewed.

Why it matters

Duration is the story. A short waiver is crisis management; a license through April 2027 is a de facto sanctions regime change for Russian refined products. It would give Moscow a year-plus revenue channel and give US fuel markets a year-plus of Russian supply — with everything that implies for Ukraine policy and European coordination.

Strategic context

US sanctions on Russian energy have been the West's central economic weapon since 2022. A long-dated license punches a hole in that regime — and hands Moscow leverage: every renewal date becomes a negotiation.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: pragmatic — markets need certainty, and a long license prevents a price cliff. Reading two: this is normalization by the back door, executed without a public policy debate. Reading three: the date may be a ceiling, not a commitment — licenses can be revoked.

Russia is supplying missiles to Iran as Tehran rebuilds its arsenal, report says

A Reuters investigation traces how Iran keeps rebuilding missile and drone stocks through a 5,000-agency procurement network importing at least $2 billion of military-related goods since January 2025, despite the US naval blockade — the latest evidence of Tehran rebuilding its arsenal.

Updated 01 Nov 2026, 15:53 UTC Verified 13 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/russia-iran-missiles-rebuild/

What happened

Russia is supplying Iran with missiles as Tehran rebuilds its arsenal, according to Bloomberg reporting cited by Haaretz on October 10, 2026. The reporting states Iran retains production capacity and substantial ballistic missile stocks despite eight months of US-Israeli airstrikes.

What is verified

  • Bloomberg reporting, cited by Haaretz (Oct 10, 2026), says Russia is supplying missiles to Iran.
  • The reporting states Iran retains ballistic missile production capacity and substantial stocks.
  • US-Israeli airstrikes against Iran have been ongoing for roughly eight months (since Feb 28, 2026).
  • Western intelligence assessed in June that Iran retained ~75% of its prewar arsenal (Bloomberg reporting, carried by Newsmax/NewsBeep, Oct 10, 2026).
  • Britain's navy says at least 10 vessels were struck near Hormuz in the past week; oil above $100/barrel; tanker charters ~$1.2M/day (same).
  • Iran reportedly test-fired multiple ballistic missiles from Tehran and Alborz provinces on Sunday Oct 11 (Türkiye Today, Oct 11, 2026; C14 News, Oct 11, 2026).
  • Bloomberg Economics analysis, carried by Türkiye Today (Oct 11, 2026), says Iran has launched more than 4,000 Shahed drones and at least 2,000 ballistic missiles since Feb 28.
  • Trump said late Monday he does not believe China or Russia are helping Iran 'at all,' telling Truth Social they have 'too much to lose' (Reuters, Oct 13, 2026) — on-record statement contradicting the Bloomberg assessment.
  • IDF raised alert level across all fronts amid reports Iran launched 30 ballistic missiles (Israel Hayom, Oct 15 — single outlet, attributed)
  • Israeli security officials: no change in Iran intelligence assessment justifying an operation in the next 2-3 weeks; Iran's missile rebuilding slow and partial (Times of Israel/Channel 12, Oct 15 — reported)
  • IRGC mass missile/drone drills Oct 11-13: ballistic-missile test-firings reported from Tehran, Alborz, Isfahan and Yazd over three days, with footage of Shahed-136 waves and IRIAF activity (Turkiye Today/The Jewish Edition/JFeed, Oct 11-13 — STRONGLY_SUPPORTED for the drills; counts per Channel 14/IRGC-linked Saberin News — ATTRIBUTED, unverified)
  • Trump pledged on social media not to attack Iran before the Nov. 3 midterm elections (Ynet carrying Bloomberg; Turkiye Today, Oct 11 — STRONGLY_SUPPORTED)
  • Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei said Tuesday that the transfer of Iran's enriched uranium material to Russia was not discussed during Sergey Lavrov's Tehran visit, and called speculation about an agreement to remove 400 kg of 60%-enriched uranium from Iran 'entirely baseless' (Trend via MENAFN; APA, Oct 20 — on-record remarks, verified); he stressed Russia's significant role in the JCPOA negotiations and Moscow's readiness to take de-escalation steps (cross-linked to Rezaei's no-exit statement, st-003 v54)
  • The Financial Times published an Oct 20, 2026 investigation (Miles Johnson, Max Seddon, Charles Clover) describing a covert Russia-Iran supersonic-missile program codenamed C430L, based on leaked correspondence, travel records and military patents; the underlying claims remain attributed pending official confirmation (relay: The Gaze, Israel Defense, Calcalist, New Vision News).
  • Oct 31 evening Tehran time (Tabnak/BitRun interview, via Al Jazeera liveblog, Clash Report, Turkiye Today): Army spokesman Akraminia says the Su-35 purchase is complete, pilot training is complete, jets were due before the war and will be received only after the war ends; no jets have arrived. VERIFIED as his remarks; no Russian confirmation or delivery detail in accounts checked.
  • Nov 1, Sunday (Reuters investigation, carried by Vijesti Nov 1 and ghall Oct 31 citing Reuters): Iran operates a network of more than 5,000 'MOD' agencies registered at its defence ministry's Tehran address, through which it has imported at least $2 billion of military-related goods since January 2025 despite the US naval blockade and Western sanctions; agency MOD 346428 alone imported rocket fuel, explosives, drone/missile components and launcher-capable trucks from January 2025 to June 2026, arriving via the Caspian from Russia, overland from Central Asia and by cargo flight from China, with shipments recorded as originating in China, Russia and Germany. VERIFIED as Reuters' findings as carried (document review with 240 Analytics, ~19,000 import records sampled, four experts confirming missile/drone usability); the dual-use caveat is carried, and no finished-missile delivery figure is claimed from this investigation.

What remains disputed

The types, quantities, and delivery timeline of the missiles have not been publicly specified. The claim that eight months of strikes failed to degrade Iran's missile capability will be contested by US and Israeli officials; no official US/Israeli assessment is reflected in the cited reporting. Russia's strategic intent — defensive resupply vs. deepening war partnership — is unstated.

Why it matters

If accurate, this is the clearest evidence yet that the US-Israeli air campaign has not achieved its stated aim of degrading Iran's strike capability — and that Moscow is willing to backfill it. That combination hardens Iran's negotiating position and lengthens the war's expected runway.

Strategic context

Russia and Iran deepened military cooperation after 2022, most visibly through Iranian drones in Ukraine. The reverse flow — Russian munitions into Iran — marks a new phase. It follows the Putin–Pezeshkian meeting in Ashgabat, where Moscow pledged mediation while visibly tightening its embrace of Tehran.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: this is routine alliance maintenance — Moscow resupplying a partner to keep the war grinding and keep Washington distracted. Reading two: it is a deterrent signal to Washington and Jerusalem ahead of any escalation. Reading three (skeptical): the reporting may overstate deliveries or conflate agreements with delivered hardware; the physical evidence is not public.

SUNDAY — REUTERS INVESTIGATION: IRAN'S 5,000-AGENCY 'MOD' PROCUREMENT NETWORK HAS IMPORTED AT LEAST $2 BILLION OF MILITARY-RELATED GOODS SINCE JANUARY 2025 DESPITE THE BLOCKADE — Reuters has identified a secret network of more than 5,000 agencies, all registered under names beginning with 'MOD' (for Iran's Ministry of Defense and Armed Forces Logistics, MODAFL) at the ministry's Tehran address, through which Iran has imported hardware, explosives and heavy trucks worth at least $2 billion since January 2025, bypassing the US naval blockade and Western sanctions (Reuters investigation, carried by Vijesti, Nov 1, 16:01 CET, and by ghall, Oct 31, citing Reuters — VERIFIED as Reuters' findings as carried; the underlying shipment records are Reuters' document review, not independently re-checked here). Reuters, working with risk consultancy 240 Analytics, based the work on thousands of Iranian banking and government documents, business-registry data and import records, reviewing a sample of about 19,000 import records; the agencies list the same Tehran address and a founding date of February 11, 1979, though documents show most were actually founded in 2013, and 36 agencies held a combined $1.2 billion in foreign-exchange accounts at the end of 2024 (same). One agency, MOD 346428, imported vast quantities of rocket fuel, explosives and weapons components needed to build drones and missiles, plus heavy trucks that can serve as mobile launchers, over the 18 months from January 2025 to June 2026; the equipment arrived by cargo ship across the Caspian Sea from Russia, by truck from Central Asia and by cargo plane from China, with a large portion of shipments recorded as originating in China, Russia and Germany (same). Four sanctions and weapons experts who analysed Reuters' findings confirmed that many of the imported materials could be used in Iran's missile and drone programmes; former senior US Treasury official Miad Maleki called the network 'the lifeblood of the Iranian military during this conflict, but also for many years before' and 'the entire ecosystem of the Iranian military's commercial operations', while Columbia researcher Peter Salisbury called it 'an industrial-scale attempt to renew or expand Iran's existing drone and missile fleet' (same). Reuters found no evidence that any of the numbered agencies in the documents had been placed on a Western sanctions list, and notes experts' dual-use caveat: a large portion of the material, including explosives, could also have civilian uses (same). Responses in the same reporting: a US Treasury spokeswoman said the US had 'cut off the regime's economic supply lines' and was 'always ten steps ahead'; China's Foreign Ministry said it was 'not familiar with the situation' and opposed attempts to 'maliciously link regional hostilities to China'; a UAE official denied the allegations about UAE-origin goods as 'completely baseless'; Iran's institutions contacted by Reuters, including its UN delegation in Geneva and the president's office, did not respond, and the Russian Defence Ministry, the White House and US Central Command did not respond to requests for comment (same). PLACEMENT: this is a procurement-system finding, not a finished-weapon delivery: it does NOT confirm v14's 327 aviation-missile figure, any Su-35 delivery in v15, or any single Russian missile shipment — shipments recorded as originating in Russia are one route among China, Germany and Central Asian overland routes, and the dual-use caveat is carried, not stripped. It does, however, materially extend the stack's core question — how Tehran keeps rebuilding missile and drone stocks while under blockade and bombardment — by documenting the financing and import machinery behind it, and it sits alongside, not instead of, the Russian-supply reporting in v12/v14. FT INVESTIGATION NAMES COVERT RUSSIA-IRAN SUPERSONIC MISSILE PROGRAM (C430L) — the Financial Times published an investigation (Miles Johnson, Max Seddon, Charles Clover; Wednesday cover story released the night of Oct 20) describing a multiyear covert program, codenamed C430L, under which Russia's state arms exporter Rosoboronexport and missile manufacturer NPO Mashinostroyenia (under U.S. sanctions since 2014) assisted Iran in developing ramjet propulsion for a new class of supersonic cruise missiles. The FT, citing leaked Russian correspondence, travel records and military patents, says the program began in 2023, a contract was signed with Iran's defense ministry in November 2023, Russian specialists — including NPO Mashinostroyenia's then-deputy head Alexander Dergachov — made repeated trips to Iran, and Iran has built and flight-tested a ramjet engine whose telemetry Russian engineers later analyzed, troubleshooting fuel supply, combustion stability, air intake and nozzle design. Missile experts cited by the FT say the technology is almost certainly intended for anti-ship and land-attack cruise missiles — a weapon class that could threaten U.S. warships, including carriers, in the Middle East — and the FT calls it 'one of the most significant known transfers of strategic military technology from Moscow to Tehran.' CLAIM STATUS: ATTRIBUTED (FT investigation) — no government confirmation from Moscow or Tehran; the reporting does not establish an operational weapon (no evidence of operational deployment at this stage). This directly challenges Trump's Oct 13 on-record denial (v7) that China or Russia are helping Iran 'at all' — claim vs counter-claim, both kept on the ledger. Relayed by The Gaze, Israel Defense, Calcalist, New Vision News. BAGHAEI DENIES URANIUM-TRANSFER TALK — Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei said at his Tuesday briefing that the transfer of Iran's enriched uranium material to Russia was NOT discussed during Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov's Tehran visit, and called speculation about an agreement to remove 400 kg of uranium enriched to 60% from Iran 'entirely baseless' (Trend via MENAFN; APA, Oct 20 — VERIFIED as Baghaei's on-record remarks).
